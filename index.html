I've been stuck to my own protective paper for years, with colors intact and glue perfectly fresh, but completely invisible at the bottom of a box. I was drawn to mark a position and bring life to a surface, but my owner's perfectionism condemned me to the safety of a dome, where I continue waiting for a perfect moment that may never come.
I was never meant to survive the evening. Just a napkin pulled from beneath a drink, marked with a quick kiss and a number scribbled before goodbye. He folded me carefully and slipped me into his pocket like I was something precious. Weeks passed before he remembered to call.
Always twisted, always pulled by the cord. Never with care. I am white, simple, essential — but never valued. This time I was left behind, coiled in some random outlet. Let's see how long she lasts without me.
I am forgotten on the dusty floor of the bus stop, heavy with the books and technology I carry on my back, but my true heart is a secret hidden in the smallest pocket. Inside, I protect an aqua-green stone, cold and smooth, which still smells of salt; we were abandoned to our fate and now share the same weight of loneliness, waiting for our straps and its colors to once again find the hands that knew how to value us.
I broke free from the straw threads and rolled through the stubble of the field, carried by a gust of wind that ripped me from my post. I spent years protecting the scarecrow from the sun and the rain, but now I am just an unraveled ball of wool on the ground, far from my owner's head, unable to scare away even the smallest of birds.
